About us

Coordinated care is how we close the gap.

Too many mothers are lost, or nearly lost, in the spaces between appointments, between providers, and between birth and recovery. Doula's House exists to close those spaces — so every mother is supported, heard and followed up with.

Our mission

To reduce maternal morbidity and mortality by putting every mother at the centre of a care team that shares one plan, speaks to each other, and stays with her through the fourth trimester.

Where care breaks down

Four gaps we're built to close

Plans that don't travel

A birth plan lives on a printout. A doula who knows exactly what a mother wants sits outside the medical record. The delivery team meets the plan — and often the mother — for the first time in labor.

How we respond: one structured plan, shared section by section with the whole team, before the due date.

Concerns that aren't heard

When a mother says something feels wrong, the response depends on whether anyone is listening — and whether anyone knows what she asked for. Many mothers, and Black mothers in particular, describe not being listened to.

How we respond: an explicit advocacy & escalation section, a direct line between doula and clinician, and a team aligned on how to respond.

The postpartum cliff

Visits taper off just when risk is high: more than half of pregnancy-related deaths happen after the first week. Warning signs get missed at home, in the night, between appointments.

How we respond: postpartum plans, doula visits and clinician check-ins built into the same shared plan — plus night-nurse support.

Support that costs too much

Doula coverage is growing but patchy; billing is hard; overnight newborn care is out of reach for many families. The mothers who'd benefit most are least able to pay.

How we respond: insurance billing for doulas, and scholarships funded by our merch store for mothers and aspiring doulas.

What the evidence says — and doesn't

Support works. We'll measure our own impact.

A Cochrane review of 21 trials (14,369 women) found continuous support during labor increased the chance of a spontaneous vaginal birth (risk ratio 1.08) and found no evidence of harm, though the certainty of the evidence was rated low.

We're careful about what we claim. Software alone doesn't change maternal mortality; coordination, continuous support and timely escalation are the levers the evidence points to. We'll track our own results, disaggregated by race, ethnicity and coverage, and publish what we learn — including what doesn't work.

Measures we commit to tracking

Plans shared with the full care team by 36 weeks
Time from a raised concern to a documented clinical response
Postpartum contact within 7 days and within 6 weeks

Equity first
