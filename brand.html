02

Identity

A doorway held open, a mother held at its centre, and a lotus rising beneath her — resilience and rebirth, standing on steady ground. No letters to misread: the idea is the mark.

The mark

The Doorway Bloom
  1. The heartThe mother — kept at the centre of the doorway, safe inside the circle of care.
  2. The lotusRising beneath her: resilience and rebirth. Rooted in mud, it blooms clean every morning — a mother’s strength, and the new beginning birth brings.
  3. The doorwayThe clinical team and the threshold we hold open — a home, a welcome. Welcome home.
  4. The groundThe doula. The steady line under everything — the one who stays, before, during and long after birth.

Construction

Grid of 100 × 100 units · stroke 11u

The mark is drawn on a 100 × 100 grid with a single 11-unit stroke. The doorway is one half-circle of r = 39.5u springing from a straight run of legs, so it reads as a clean, friendly arch even at 16 pixels. The ground line is 6u — lighter than the doorway, because it supports rather than frames.

Everything sits on the vertical axis. The heart is centred at 34u; the lotus is planted 11u above the baseline and rises 38u. The three elements are separated by clear air — never touching — so the mark stays legible at small sizes.

The lotus has five petals cut apart by a 2.4u gap. Its gaps are true cut-outs, not paint, so the mark works on any colour or photograph.

The heart and the lotus are the only elements that take a second colour in the signature version — both Clay, because both belong to the mother.

Why a lotus

Resilience & rebirth

The lotus grows from mud, rises through dark water and opens clean in the light — and does it again every morning. In many cultures it carries the idea of resilience and rebirth: exactly what mothers show, and what birth gives.

Inside the mark, the lotus is planted in the doorway beneath the heart. On its own, the lotus emblem is our small mark: embroidered pockets, patches, stamps, favicons-with-attitude. It never replaces the mark in a lockup, and always keeps its five cut petals.

Colour

A warm, earthy base — studio mocha, linen and hearth — with three strand colours that carry meaning across the whole system.

Primary palette

Named like things you’d find in a home

Supporting

Surfaces, depth and states

Proportion

Warm, calm, and mostly quiet

Paper and Cream carry most surfaces; Hearth and Umber carry text and depth; Clay, Honey and Dawn are accents — never large flat fills except Clay in campaign moments.

Colour as meaning

The strands

Wherever the product shows people — avatars, chips, charts, messages — a mother’s colour is Clay, a doula’s is Honey, a clinician’s is Dawn. The spectrum version of the mark is the same idea in a single glyph.

Accessible pairings

WCAG contrast, computed

Dawn and Honey are graphic colours: use them for fills, marks and chips with dark text on top — never as text on light surfaces (use Dawn deep or Umber). On Hearth, use the light variants of Clay and Dawn.

05

Graphic language

A few simple devices, repeated with discipline. Each one comes from the mark.

The arch frame. Portraits live in arches — the doorway, made visible. One per layout.
Threshold arcs. Three strokes of graded weight (2 · 3.5 · 5) radiating from a corner or edge. Always cropped, never complete.
The triple rule. The divider: three lines, 1.5 · 2.5 · 3.5 — the same weights as the arcs.

Layout, space & shape

8px base · 12 columns

Content sits on a 12-column grid inside a 1200px container, with 20–40px gutters. Space is built in an 8px rhythm (4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 72 · 112). Generous section padding is part of the voice: unhurried.

8 · 18 · 28 · pill · arch — pills for actions, arches for people.

Iconography

1.7px stroke · 24px grid · round caps

Motion

Calm, rising, never bouncy

Things arrive the way a door opens: they rise and settle. Standard easing is cubic-bezier(.2, .7, .2, 1), duration 600–1100ms for reveals and 180ms for interactions. The arch reveal above is the signature motion; all animation is removed under prefers-reduced-motion.

06

Imagery

Dignified, direct, warm. Mothers are never props; they are the point of view.

Direct gaze. She looks at us as an equal.
Warm studio light. Soft, directional, never clinical white.

Do

  • Real mothers, doulas and clinicians, photographed with consent
  • Black and brown mothers at the centre — not the margin
  • Warm, lifted shadows; mocha, camel and cream backdrops
  • Crop into the arch; keep the chin and hands

Don’t

  • Stock-photo clichés: stethoscopes on bellies, sunsets, hearts-in-hands
  • Faceless torsos as a default
  • Cold blue-white hospital lighting
  • Filters that flatten skin tone

Voice

We talk like the best doula you know: calm, specific, on your side.

Plain.

Short sentences. Everyday words. Say it the way you’d say it across a kitchen table.

Specific.

“Dr. Okafor can see 6 of 10 sections.” Not “enhanced visibility.”

On her side.

Mothers decide. We explain, we ask, we never lecture or scare.

Architecture

One house, many rooms. A single master brand with plain-spoken, endorsed lines.

Master brand
Doula’s House CareThe platform

Birth plan, care team, messages, billing. The product behind sign-in.

Doula’s House FundScholarships & giving

Doula support, night-nurse care, baby prep and doula training.

Doula’s House ShopMerchandise

Wear it, gift it, fund it. Every order feeds the Fund.

Doula’s House CircleCommunity & stories

Stories, the doula network and doula-aligned practices.

Naming rules

  • Always “Doula’s House” + one plain word
  • The line word takes an accent colour; the wordmark never changes
  • Endorsed, not independent: the master lockup always appears

Avoid

  • Invented sub-brand names or new marks per line
  • Acronyms (DHC, DHF) in public copy
  • Using “Doula-aligned” as a product — it’s a seal
