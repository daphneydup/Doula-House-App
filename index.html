The gap

Doulas live outside the medical field. Mothers feel it.

Ask a doula

Please don't include medical details. Demo answers only.

What does a doula actually do?
A doula is a trained, non-medical professional who gives continuous emotional, physical and informational support before, during and after birth. Doulas don't deliver babies or give clinical advice — they help you feel prepared, heard and supported, and help you communicate with your care team.
Will my doctor or hospital actually work with a doula?
That's the point of Doula's House. Providers and hospitals can publish a doula-aligned profile — doula access in labor & delivery, acceptance of shared birth plans, an escalation pathway and a postpartum hand-off — so you can choose a team that has already said yes.
Does insurance pay for doulas?
Some Medicaid programs and private plans do, and the list is growing, but coverage varies by state and plan. We help you check coverage, and help your doula assemble and submit a clean claim. Where coverage isn't available, scholarships can help.
Who can see my birth plan?
Only the people you choose, section by section, for the time you choose. Clinical details (like health history) can only be shared with clinical roles. You can see an audit log of every access and revoke it at any moment.
How do scholarships work?
Merch sales and donations fund three tracks: doula support for moms, postpartum night-nurse care, and training scholarships for aspiring doulas. Applications are reviewed by our team — and you should never include medical details in them.
Is Doula's House medical advice?
No. We coordinate care and communication; we don't diagnose or treat. In an emergency, call 911.
